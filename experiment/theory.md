### Theory

Viral RNA/DNA extraction is the process of isolating the viral genome from clinical, laboratory, or field samples, such as serum, plasma, cell culture supernatant, or tissue homogenates [1]. Viral nucleic acids are generally encapsulated within protein capsids, so efficient lysis is crucial for isolating and purifying them.

The most common steps in the extraction process of viral RNA/DNA are:
* **Chemical lysis of viral particles:** Lysis buffers contain chaotropic salts such as guanidinium thiocyanate and detergents, which disrupt the viral envelope and capsid, releasing the nucleic acids into solution.
* **Inactivation of nucleases:** The chaotropic salts also denature proteins, including RNases and DNases, thereby preventing degradation of viral RNA or DNA during extraction.
* **Binding of nucleic acid to silica membrane:** Viral RNA/DNA selectively binds to a silica membrane or magnetic beads present in spin columns.
* **Washing to remove contaminants:** Multiple wash steps using ethanol-based buffers to remove proteins, salts, lipids, and other inhibitors.
* **Elution of purified RNA/DNA:** Nuclease-free water is used to elute the purified viral RNA/DNA from the membrane.

### Quantification of Viral RNA/DNA
The concentration and purity of the extracted viral nucleic acids can be assessed using spectrophotometric method. For good-quality nucleic acid, the A260/A280 ratio should be ~2.0 for RNA and ~1.8 for DNA, indicating minimal protein contamination. Accurate quantification ensures the reliability and reproducibility of downstream applications such as PCR, cloning, viral titration analysis, and sequencing [2, 3].
